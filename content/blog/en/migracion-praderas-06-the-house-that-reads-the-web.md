In the [previous chapter](/blog/en/migracion-praderas-05-hands-on-the-house) the house already had trades: watch, brief, publish, move a card. One trade still looked innocent and was not: **looking at the web**.

Because as soon as the agent “searches,” someone else decides what it sees. The index, the ranking, the snippet, sometimes the training. The move is not finished on your own disk if the daily gaze is rented out entirely.

### The what: reading with a perimeter

This is not inventing a search engine. It is letting the house **ask the web and bring sources**, with a clear plan:

- A **local** path that orchestrates search and reading from your machine or server, without an account at a single giant.
- A **spare key** when that path fails or falls short — a provider with an API you already use, not a maze of sign-ups.
- A **morning brief** that does not confuse “news from three weeks ago” with yesterday, and that also knows **what you have today**: agenda, tasks, whether the disk is tight, whether the blog is current.

That is the what. The house does not “know the internet.” The house **reads with judgment** and leaves it in the same pocket chat.

### The why: the gaze is sovereignty too

Talking to your cloud was the threshold. Giving it hands was the next stretch. Search is where soft dependence sneaks in: one free service, one ranking, one “summary” that hides the link.

The why is domestic. You want to hear what matters in your world — own cloud, tools, privacy — without breakfast being a three-week newspaper or a marketing thread. And you want the agent, if it says “done,” to have looked at **sources**, not invented headlines.

There is a practical why too. The brief is not only web. It is **your day**: if the train is at seven, if a task is still open, if the server wants updates. The web without house context is noise. The house without the web goes short-sighted. Together, in one short message, they are a habit.

### The how: two doors and a morning ritual

The how is not a brand list. It is an arrangement.

**First door: close to home.** A program of your own on the server (or the laptop) that searches and brings pages as clean text. It runs where you say. It does not replace the internet — it still goes out to the public web — but **you** choose the engine and the perimeter. If a bot wall gets hard one day, that is not the end: there is a second step.

**Second door: the spare key.** A search provider with a real key, paid or with a serious quota, that steps in when the first door is not enough. It is not the owner of the gaze; it is plan B. So the trade does not die the day the local path coughs.

**The ritual.** On weekdays, at a fixed hour, the house gathers three things before it opines:

1. **Today** — what is on the calendar and what is still open in tasks.  
2. **House** — what the server can say without drama: space, pending updates, whether the blog matches what was already merged.  
3. **Web** — only what smells like the **last hours**, not the archive. Few pieces. Title, one sentence, the link.

If nothing fresh is on the web, it says so. If the agenda is free, it says so too. Honest silence beats elegant filler.

And when blog text is already on the main branch, publishing remains the trade of pulling forward and checking that the site answers — not rewriting history on the server.

### How it feels

It is not a control panel. It is the same chat as always, in the morning: a paragraph of your day, a mild note from the house, two or three links worth a look. No tables. No “as an AI…”. No prompt leaking into the message.

Sometimes the web brings nothing new. That is an adult answer too.

### Closing

The move is about not giving away the keys. It is also about not giving away the gaze.

Talking to the house, giving it hands, and teaching it to **read the web with judgment** are the same thread: everyday autonomy, concrete trades, proof when it says “done.” Mail and other pieces can wait. For now the house answers, watches, briefs, publishes — and in the morning it leaves a brief that knows **your** day, not only the internet’s.
