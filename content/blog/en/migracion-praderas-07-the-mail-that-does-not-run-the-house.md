We can hate email with all the dignity in the world. It does not matter. Bookings, schools, insurers, “please confirm,” PDFs nobody knows where they put them… **still arrive there**. Moving the house does not erase that collective habit. If the agent only lives in chat, a slice of real life stays outside.

In earlier chapters the house already talks, has hands, and reads the web with judgment. Something less glamorous and more practical was still missing: a **narrow mailbox** where you forward what matters and get an answer in the same thread.

### The what: a channel, not a butler with the keys

This is not “the agent runs my email.” That is another trade, and another risk. It is this:

- An address **of its own** (the agent’s), not your personal inbox mixed in.
- Only **you** can write to it (for now).
- It only **answers you**, in-thread.
- It is for **general questions** and for **evaluating** what you forward: a confusing message, a long notice, a “what are they actually asking?”.
- It does **not** open the door to house automation. That door is still the chat on the phone. Mail does not move calendars, publish the blog, or touch the server “because the subject line said so.”

That is the what: email joins the **same stream** as the agent — reading, judgment, reply — without handing over the remote.

### The why: time leaks in the thread, not in the magic

The why is not love of SMTP. It is realism.

Every day someone forwards you something “to look at.” You open it, read it twice, hunt the paragraph that matters, half-reply, leave it for later. Multiply by travel, schools, and paperwork, and it is a **ton of minutes**.

If you can forward to the agent and, a few minutes later, have a clear summary, the risk, the missing question, or a calm “this is just noise”… you have not invented the future. You have **removed friction** from a habit you will not abolish.

There is a why of limits too. Mixing “answer this” with “wipe the database” in the same mailbox is tempting and dangerous. Separating **questions** (mail) from **house trades** (Talk) is the same idea as always: hands yes, remote no. Mail is one more path into the conversation; it is not the master switch.

And a mild sovereignty why: you do not need to move the whole family’s mail to try the habit. The agent’s box can live where mail already lives. The big mail-server move can wait for a quiet week. The time savings need not.

### The how: narrow on purpose

The how fits in an arrangement, not a manual.

1. **A dedicated address** for the agent.  
2. **A short list of who may speak to it** — today, your address. Everyone else: silence.  
3. **Reply in the same thread**, like a polite person.  
4. **A bounded trade**: explain, summarize, orient. If the house needs automation, it says so and points you to the automation chat.  
5. **No theater about moving mail**: you do not need a new mail server for this gesture. Later, if you move mail, you move the address with the rest.

Technical details — protocols, intervals, formats — matter to whoever builds the house. What matters to the rest is the gesture: **forward → judgment → reply**, without the inbox becoming a control panel in disguise.

(The agent still answers in plain text. Pretty HTML mail will come when the software matures; that is not the core of the habit.)

### How it feels

It is not a second brain living in Gmail. It is humbler: you open the phone at the airport, forward the hotel or school thread, and go back to your life. When you look again, there is an answer for you — to you — that spares the full second reading.

Sometimes it says “this is unclear; ask X.” That saves time too.

### Closing

Life stays anchored to email even if we dislike it. Bringing **that** flow to the agent is not surrendering to the past: it is stopping the pretense that everything fits in one chat.

Talking to the house, giving it hands, reading the web, and now **forwarding without fear of losing an afternoon**… are the same thread. Mail does not run the house. It just stops being a dead end.
