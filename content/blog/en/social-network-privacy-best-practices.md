Social networks are woven into daily life—for better and worse. As more personal data lives online, **privacy on social platforms** deserves deliberate attention. This article collects practical habits that reduce risk without pretending the problem disappears.

## 1. Review privacy settings

Each network exposes knobs for who can see posts, tag you, or send friend requests. Revisit them after major UI updates—defaults often drift toward “more public.”

## 2. Share selectively

You do not owe the internet every detail. Think twice before posting addresses, phone numbers, or precise real-time location.

## 3. Use strong passwords

Unique, complex passwords per account. Avoid birthdays and pet names—those are easy to guess or scrape from other posts.

## 4. Vet friend requests

Accept connections from people you actually know. Fake profiles remain a common pretext for scams and data harvesting.

## 5. Tags and mentions

Require approval before tagged posts appear on your profile when the platform allows it—tags can leak context you did not intend to broadcast.

## 6. Direct messages are not “private vaults”

Anything sent online can be screenshotted or forwarded. Treat DMs as **semi-public** unless you use end-to-end encrypted channels built for sensitive content.

## 7. Keep apps updated

Updates routinely ship security fixes; delaying them increases exposure.

## 8. Review connected apps

Revoke OAuth or third-party integrations you no longer use—old tokens are forgotten attack surface.

## 9. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)

Where available, 2FA is one of the highest ROI controls for account takeover.

## 10. Curate your network over time

As relationships change, pruning followers/friends reduces who can see historical posts under evolving defaults.

## Conclusion

Privacy on social networks is **proactive work**: settings, sharing discipline, and authentication hygiene matter more than any single “privacy mode” marketing label. You can still participate—just choose consciously what you share, with whom, and through which apps.

Every post adds to a **digital footprint**; informed choices keep that footprint closer to the story you want told about you.
